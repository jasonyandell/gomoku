# Loss Floor Bouncing In Small-Scale AlphaZero

Maintained interpretation page for the recurring pattern where policy/total
loss reaches a low floor, bumps upward, then returns to the floor while fixed
external baselines improve.

## Short Answer

This is not a known bug signature by itself. In this repo, the default reading
is now:

> loss-floor bouncing is expected small-scale AlphaZero turbulence when the
> self-play distribution and the MCTS target policy are still moving.

It becomes a bug suspicion only when the bounce comes with extra evidence:
NaN/Inf metrics, worker death, poisoned policy vectors, illegal-action mass,
value loss collapsing to a trivial constant while plies collapse, or sustained
external-baseline regression across several eval windows.

## Why Loss Can Bounce While Strength Improves

`gomoku.train.policy_loss` is cross-entropy against the MCTS visit
distribution:

```
policy_loss = -sum(pi_mcts * log p_net)
```

That is not a fixed validation loss. The target `pi_mcts` is generated by the
current self-play/search system, so the teacher changes whenever the model
discovers a new line, a new defense, or a new way to force longer games.

Mathematically, the policy cross-entropy is:

```
H(pi_mcts) + KL(pi_mcts || p_net)
```

The irreducible floor `H(pi_mcts)` changes as MCTS visit distributions sharpen
or spread out. The `KL` part bumps when the replay buffer suddenly contains
positions whose MCTS targets are not yet what the raw net predicts. If training
absorbs the new regime, loss falls again.

That "bump, absorb, lower floor" shape is exactly what we want to see when it
is paired with longer games and stronger fixed-baseline play.

## What Is Documented Outside This Repo

I do not know a standard paper term for "loss bounces off the bottom" in
AlphaZero. The documented pieces do support this interpretation:

- AlphaGo Zero/AlphaZero train the network on `(state, MCTS policy, game
  outcome)` generated by the current or recent self-play system; the target is
  a moving policy-improvement operator, not a stationary dataset.
- AlphaZero at published scale used thousands of TPUs for self-play and many
  training TPUs, which smooths over per-version quirks that our 8-worker laptop
  setup sees directly.
- Small-game AlphaZero studies report high sensitivity to hyperparameters and
  cases where more training can lower performance, so non-monotonic curves are
  not surprising at small scale.

Useful primary anchors:

- [Silver et al. 2017, *Mastering the game of Go without human
  knowledge*](https://gwern.net/doc/reinforcement-learning/model/alphago/2017-silver.pdf):
  MCTS search probabilities are the policy target; self-play/search is the
  policy-evaluation and policy-improvement loop.
- [Silver et al. 2018, *A general reinforcement learning algorithm that masters
  chess, shogi, and Go through
  self-play*](https://gwern.net/doc/reinforcement-learning/model/alphago/2018-silver.pdf):
  AlphaZero continually updates a single network and generates self-play from
  latest parameters; published scale used 5000 TPUs for self-play and 16 TPUs
  for training.
- [Wu 2020, *Accelerating Self-Play Learning in Go*
  (KataGo)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10565): several laptop-relevant
  stability/efficiency lessons, especially playout cap randomization, policy
  target pruning, global context, auxiliary targets, increasing replay window,
  SWA/EMA-style candidate nets, and gating.
- [Trudeau and Bowling 2023, *Targeted Search Control in AlphaZero for
  Effective Policy Improvement*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12359): standard
  initial-state AlphaZero can under-explore deeper game-tree states; starting
  some self-play from archived states improves sample efficiency in Connect Four
  and 9x9 Go.
- [Soemers et al. 2019, *Learning Policies from Self-Play with Policy Gradients
  and MCTS Value Estimates*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05809): MCTS
  visit-count imitation via cross-entropy is the common strong approach, but
  because MCTS explores, that objective also trains exploratory behavior into
  the policy.
- [Wang, Emmerich, Preuss, and Plaat 2020, *Analysis of Hyper-Parameters for
  Small Games*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.05988): AlphaZero-like small-game
  training is highly sensitive, and more training can sometimes reduce
  performance.
- [Lees and Matiisen 2026, *Reproducing AlphaZero on
  Tablut*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05476): asymmetric AlphaZero reproduction saw
  training instability/catastrophic forgetting between roles; mitigations
  included augmentation, larger replay, and 25% games against sampled past
  checkpoints.

## Next-Run Lessons From The Sources

The highest-order lesson is: do not optimize for a pretty loss curve. Optimize
for target quality, position coverage, and fixed-opponent strength. A bouncy
policy loss can be a sign that the teacher moved, not that learning broke.

Concrete lessons for the next run:

1. **Add fixed validation probes.** Current `loss/policy` is on a moving
   self-play distribution. Add a frozen validation archive and log policy CE/KL
   there: old WL3/WL4 positions, heuristic-loss positions, lookahead-loss
   positions, long-defense positions, high-loss/high-KL positions, and maybe
   canonical opening positions. This separates "model got worse" from "current
   self-play target distribution changed."
2. **Split policy loss into target entropy plus KL.** Log `H(pi_mcts)`, net
   policy entropy, and `KL(pi_mcts || p_net)` separately. If total policy loss
   bumps because `H(pi_mcts)` changed, that is a target-shape event. If KL
   spikes and does not recover, that is a learning/stability event.
3. **Keep scale substitutes.** EMA self-play, past-checkpoint mix, gradient
   accumulation, careful replay size/age, and external fixed baselines are not
   incidental. They substitute for the smoothing AlphaZero got from thousands
   of TPUs, huge batches, and huge replay.
4. **Prefer structured start diversity over crude opening randomness.**
   Go-Exploit argues for starting some trajectories from archived interesting
   states. For Gomoku, replace or supplement `random_opening_moves` with a
   small fraction of games starting from curated trouble states: long defenses,
   high-KL positions, missed blocks, canonical-line branches, and external
   opponent losses. Keep most games canonical so the run still answers the
   real opening question.
5. **Decouple exploration from policy labels.** KataGo's target-pruning lesson
   is directly relevant: exploration visits can be useful for search but noisy
   as policy labels. A future ablation should prune or downweight forced/noisy
   exploratory visits so the network learns what search concluded, not every
   exploratory poke.
6. **Instrument role asymmetry.** Freestyle 9x9 has first-player advantage,
   and the Tablut paper is a warning that role conflicts can look like
   forgetting. Before changing architecture, log evals by color, value error by
   side-to-move/ply bucket, and role-conditioned fixed-probe loss.

## Candidate Next-Run Shape

Do measurement first, then change one behavioral lever.

Baseline: WL4 recipe, plus diagnostics:

- fixed validation archive loss/KL;
- `H(pi_mcts)`, net entropy, and policy KL;
- color/ply-bucket value and policy metrics;
- existing fixed external baselines and plies metrics.

First behavioral lever if WL4 keeps bouncing without improving:

- archive-start diversity: 10-25% of self-play games begin from a curated
  archive of interesting states, while the remaining 75-90% remain canonical
  K=0 from the empty board.

Why this lever first: it targets the exact source-backed failure mode that
initial-state self-play can under-cover deeper or rarer states, without
reintroducing fully random openings as permanent training infrastructure.

## Bug Triage Rule

Treat loss-floor bouncing as healthy if most of these are true:

- `loss/value` is finite and not collapsing to a trivial near-zero value while
  games also collapse to very short plies.
- `selfplay/plies_p90` or `selfplay/plies_mean` is stable/growing.
- Fixed external baselines are noisy but not suffering sustained multi-window
  collapse.
- `train/policy_acc` recovers after each bump.
- Worker logs are quiet and no NaN policy sanitizers are firing repeatedly.

Investigate a bug if any of these happen:

- NaN/Inf in loss, MCTS policy, or checkpoint.
- Workers die or a wave barrier stalls.
- A loss bump coincides with plies dropping back into 5-15 move fast-attack
  collapse and staying there.
- External baselines regress for several eval windows while loss also rises.
- Replay-buffer stats abruptly show impossible shape or corrupted target mix.

## Current Working Theory

WL4 suggests that `K=2` opening diversity was useful infrastructure for
building the representation, but not a permanent crutch. Removing it caused a
real policy-target distribution shock, then the model absorbed the canonical
opening distribution and found a lower loss floor while retaining external
strength.

The remaining floor-bounce is probably the model cycling through new local
canonical-line discoveries: MCTS finds a sharper or different policy, the net
chases it, self-play composition shifts, then the loss floor moves again.
